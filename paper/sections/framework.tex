\section{Spectral Framework for Pretraining Monitoring}
\label{sec:framework}
%=============================================================================

We define two primary spectral metrics that together provide a complete ``instrument panel'' for pretraining: the \emph{normalized stable rank} (SR/$d$), which measures compression progress, and the \emph{power-law exponent} ($\alpha$), which measures structural quality. Both are computable from weights alone, without training data or gradient statistics.

\subsection{Metric Definitions}
\label{sec:metrics}

\paragraph{Stable rank (SR).} For a weight matrix $W \in \R^{m \times n}$ with singular values $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \cdots$, the stable rank is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{SR}(W) = \frac{\|W\|_F^2}{\|W\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2} = \frac{\sum_i \sigma_i^2}{\sigma_1^2}.
    \label{eq:stable_rank}
\end{equation}
This measures the \emph{effective dimensionality}: how many ``$\sigma_1$-sized'' directions the matrix uses. For a rank-1 matrix, $\mathrm{SR} = 1$; for a matrix with $k$ equal singular values, $\mathrm{SR} = k$. Crucially, stable rank only requires the Frobenius norm ($O(mn)$) and the top singular value (power iteration, $O(mn)$)---no full SVD needed.

\paragraph{Normalized stable rank (SR/$d$).} We average the stable rank across all 2D weight layers and normalize by the hidden dimension $d$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{SR}/d = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{l=1}^L \frac{\mathrm{SR}(W_l)}{d},
    \label{eq:sr_norm}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the number of measured layers and $d$ is the model's hidden dimension. This normalization makes the metric comparable across architectures with different $d$.

\paragraph{Power-law exponent ($\alpha$).} Following Martin \& Mahoney~\citep{martin_mahoney_jmlr2021}, we fit the empirical spectral density (ESD) of each layer's eigenvalues ($\lambda_i = \sigma_i^2$) to a power law $P(\lambda) \sim \lambda^{-\alpha}$ using maximum likelihood estimation with KS-statistic-based $x_{\min}$ selection. The global $\alpha$ is the parameter-weighted average across layers.

The power-law exponent characterizes the spectral structure:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
    \item $\alpha > 6$: \textbf{Random} (Marchenko-Pastur bulk, no learned structure)
    \item $\alpha \in [4, 6]$: \textbf{Bulk + Spikes} (partially trained, isolated features)
    \item $\alpha \in [2, 4]$: \textbf{Heavy-Tail} (well-trained, pervasive self-regularization)
    \item $\alpha < 2$: \textbf{Rank collapse} (over-trained, potential failure mode)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Supplementary metrics.} We additionally track:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Top-10 concentration} $C_{10}$: fraction of total variance in the 10 largest singular values, $C_{10} = \sum_{i=1}^{10}\sigma_i^2 / \sum_j \sigma_j^2$.
    \item \textbf{Layer-type decomposition}: $\alpha_{\mathrm{attn}}$ vs.\ $\alpha_{\mathrm{mlp}}$ to identify which component is the bottleneck.
    \item \textbf{$\alpha$ velocity}: $d\alpha/dt$, the rate of structural change per training step.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Theoretical Grounding}
\label{sec:theory}

\paragraph{Why stable rank measures compression.}
At initialization (e.g., Kaiming/Xavier), weight matrices are approximately Gaussian with $\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{init}} \approx d/2.5$ (from Marchenko-Pastur theory: $\mathrm{SR} \approx mn / (\sqrt{m} + \sqrt{n})^2$). During training, learned structure concentrates variance in fewer directions, reducing SR. The ratio $\mathrm{SR}/\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{init}} = \mathrm{SR}/(0.4d)$ is the \emph{Universal Compression Ratio} (UCR): what fraction of the initial effective dimensionality the model retains after training.

\paragraph{Why $\alpha$ measures structural quality.}
Martin \& Mahoney~\citep{martin_mahoney_jmlr2021} showed that SGD with weight decay produces implicit self-regularization that drives eigenvalue distributions toward heavy tails. The exponent $\alpha$ quantifies the degree of this self-regularization: lower $\alpha$ means stronger correlations between learned features, more efficient use of representational capacity, and empirically better generalization. This is the spectral manifestation of the ``compression-as-understanding'' principle~\citep{freedman2026}.

\paragraph{Connection to thermodynamics.}
In the framework of \citet{sadrtdinov2025}, SGD minimizes free energy $\mathcal{F} = U - T\mathcal{S}$. The stable rank is directly related to the spectral entropy $\mathcal{S}$: $\log(\mathrm{SR}) \approx \mathcal{S}$ (since $\mathrm{SR} = \exp(\mathcal{S})$ for the Rényi-2 entropy of normalized singular values). Thus SR/$d$ decreasing during training corresponds to entropy decreasing---the system ordering. The $\alpha$ exponent characterizes the \emph{quality} of this ordering: heavy-tail ($\alpha < 4$) corresponds to a ``crystal'' (structured, generalizing); bulk+spikes ($\alpha > 5$) corresponds to a ``glass'' (partially ordered, brittle).

\subsection{Key Predictions}
\label{sec:predictions}

The framework yields four testable predictions:

\noindent\textbf{P1 (Universal compression).} $\mathrm{SR}/d$ converges to a constant $\approx 0.04$--$0.07$ regardless of model scale $N$ or architecture.
\emph{Falsified if}: SR/$d$ varies by $>50\%$ across scales or architectures.

\noindent\textbf{P2 (Performance prediction).} SR/$d$ has stronger rank correlation with downstream performance than any single scalar extractable from the loss curve.
\emph{Falsified if}: Spearman $|r| < 0.7$.

\noindent\textbf{P3 ($\alpha$ reversal).} For models with $D/N < 300$, $\alpha$ reaches a minimum then \emph{increases}, signaling structural degradation.
\emph{Falsified if}: $\alpha$ monotonically decreases for all models regardless of $D/N$.

\noindent\textbf{P4 (Structural Chinchilla).} $\alpha_{\mathrm{final}}$ follows $\alpha = \alpha_\infty + A\exp(-D/\tau N)$ with $\tau \approx 250$--$300$.
\emph{Falsified if}: no monotone relationship between $D/N$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{final}}$.
