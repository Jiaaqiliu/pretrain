\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%=============================================================================

\paragraph{Heavy-tail self-regularization.}
Martin \& Mahoney~\citep{martin_mahoney_jmlr2021} discovered that well-trained neural network layers exhibit heavy-tailed eigenvalue spectra and that the power-law exponent $\alpha$ correlates with model quality. Their WeightWatcher tool~\citep{weightwatcher} demonstrated this across hundreds of pre-trained models. Our work extends their framework from \emph{static} analysis of final models to \emph{dynamic} monitoring during training, discovering the $\alpha$ reversal phenomenon and the Structural Chinchilla scaling law.

\paragraph{Stable rank in deep learning.}
Sanyal et al.~\citep{sanyal2019stable} proposed Stable Rank Normalization as a regularizer, showing that constraining stable rank improves generalization. We use stable rank as a \emph{monitoring metric} rather than a regularizer, and discover its universal convergence (SR/$d \approx 0.055$) across architectures---a finding not previously reported.

\paragraph{Thermodynamics of neural networks.}
Several works establish thermodynamic analogies for training: \citet{sadrtdinov2025} showed SGD minimizes free energy; \citet{liu_tegmark2025} identified learning rate with temperature via fluctuation-dissipation; \citet{pvnkt_ijcai2026} verified PV=NkT at small scale. Our initial investigation attempted to scale these state-equation results to 7B+, but found the PV/(NT) formulation does not converge at scale (CV $> 40\%$). This motivated our shift to spectral metrics (SR, $\alpha$) which provide robust, scale-invariant diagnostics.

\paragraph{Chinchilla scaling laws.}
Hoffmann et al.~\citep{hoffmann2022} established compute-optimal data/parameter ratios ($D/N \approx 20$). Subsequent work showed that ``over-training'' beyond Chinchilla ratios produces better models for deployment~\citep{gadre2024, llama3}. Our Structural Chinchilla law provides a spectral explanation: structural maturity ($\alpha < 3$) requires $D/N > 500$, far exceeding compute-optimality.

\paragraph{Training monitoring.}
Current practice monitors loss, gradient norms, and occasionally loss spikes~\citep{deepseekv3}. \citet{grokking_pretraining2025} showed MoE routing consistency predicts performance at $r > 0.97$ but this only applies to mixture-of-experts models. Our SR/$d$ metric ($r = -0.92$) works for all dense transformers and requires only weight access, not routing statistics.

\paragraph{Random Matrix Theory for neural networks.}
The Marchenko-Pastur distribution~\citep{marchenko_pastur1967} describes eigenvalue spectra of random matrices. Deviations from MP signal learned structure. Pennington \& Worah~\citep{pennington2017} analyzed spectra of trained networks via free probability theory. Our work uses deviation from MP (quantified by $\alpha$) as a real-time training signal rather than a post-hoc analysis tool.
