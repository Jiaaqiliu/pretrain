\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%=============================================================================

\paragraph{Heavy-tail self-regularization.}
Martin \& Mahoney~\citep{martin_mahoney_jmlr2021} discovered that well-trained neural network layers exhibit heavy-tailed eigenvalue spectra and that the power-law exponent $\alpha$ correlates with model quality. Their WeightWatcher tool~\citep{weightwatcher} demonstrated this across hundreds of pre-trained models. Our work extends their framework from \emph{static} analysis of final models to \emph{dynamic} monitoring during training, discovering the $\alpha$ reversal phenomenon, the three-phase dynamics, and the Structural Chinchilla scaling law. We also introduce SR/$d$ as a complementary metric with stronger cross-scale correlation ($r = -0.92$) than $\alpha$ alone.

\paragraph{Stable rank in deep learning.}
Sanyal et al.~\citep{sanyal2019stable} proposed Stable Rank Normalization as a regularizer. We use stable rank as a \emph{monitoring metric} rather than a regularizer, and establish its universal convergence (SR/$d \approx 0.040 + 0.61/\sqrt{d}$) across architectures, a finding not previously reported. We further show that this convergence corresponds to a universal R\'{e}nyi-2 entropy reduction of ${\sim}2$ nats.

\paragraph{Thermodynamics of neural networks.}
Several works establish thermodynamic frameworks for training: \citet{sadrtdinov2025} showed SGD minimizes free energy; \citet{liu_tegmark2025} identified learning rate with temperature via fluctuation-dissipation; \citet{pvnkt_ijcai2026} verified PV=NkT at small scale. Our contribution is not a new state equation (the PV/NT formulation does not converge at scale), but rather the identification of \emph{spectral} thermodynamic quantities, specifically SR as $\exp(H_2)$ and $\alpha$ as a measure of the ordered/disordered phase, that provide robust diagnostics where macroscopic state equations fail.

\paragraph{Chinchilla scaling laws.}
Hoffmann et al.~\citep{hoffmann2022} established compute-optimal data/parameter ratios ($D/N \approx 20$). Subsequent work showed that over-training beyond Chinchilla ratios produces better models~\citep{gadre2024, llama3}. Our Structural Chinchilla law provides a spectral explanation: structural maturity ($\alpha < 3$) requires $D/N > 500$, far exceeding compute-optimality. We show that the maturation timescale $\tau$ grows with model size, explaining why frontier models remain structurally immature despite massive absolute token counts.

\paragraph{Training monitoring.}
Current practice monitors loss, gradient norms, and occasionally loss spikes~\citep{deepseekv3}. Our SR/$d$ metric ($r = -0.92$) works for all dense transformers and requires only weight access, not routing statistics or evaluation data.

\paragraph{Random Matrix Theory.}
The Marchenko-Pastur distribution~\citep{marchenko_pastur1967} describes eigenvalue spectra of random matrices. Deviations from MP signal learned structure. Pennington \& Worah~\citep{pennington2017} analyzed spectra via free probability theory. Our work uses MP deviation (quantified by $\alpha$ and SR/$d$) as a real-time signal rather than a post-hoc analysis tool, and identifies the universal convergence point $\text{SR}/d \to 0.04$ as $d \to \infty$.
