\paragraph{Result.} SR/$d$ alone explains 75.4\% of downstream performance variance across all models and training stages, without requiring evaluation data. Adding model size ($\log N$) as a second predictor improves to $R^2 = 0.878$.

\paragraph{Regression formula.}
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{score} \approx -0.258 \cdot \log_{10}(\mathrm{SR}/d) + 0.048 \cdot \log_{10}(N) - 0.252
    \label{eq:regression}
\end{equation}
This enables real-time performance prediction during training without running expensive evaluation suites.

\subsection{P3: $\alpha$ Reversal---Detecting Structural Degradation}
\label{sec:reversal}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Observation.} For models with insufficient tokens per parameter, the power-law exponent $\alpha$ reaches a minimum early in training, then \emph{increases}---signaling that spectral structure is \emph{degrading} even as loss continues to decrease. Critically, the OLMo-2-13B result reveals that the reversal threshold is \emph{model-size-dependent}: despite $D/N = 365$ (above the ${\sim}250$ threshold inferred from smaller models), 13B exhibits the largest reversal of any model measured ($\Delta\alpha = +2.71$).

\paragraph{Mechanism: MLP layers drive the reversal.}
Layer-type analysis reveals that $\alpha_{\mathrm{attn}}$ stabilizes early and remains flat, while $\alpha_{\mathrm{mlp}}$ continues rising after the global minimum. MLP layers store factual knowledge and have higher capacity demands; with insufficient training data, gradient noise from new diverse data destabilizes partially-learned MLP structure while attention patterns remain robust. The MLP/Attention gap (defined as $\alpha_{\mathrm{mlp}} - \alpha_{\mathrm{attn}}$) grows monotonically with model size, reaching 1.69 at 13B---the largest of all models measured---confirming that MLP is the structural bottleneck and that the gap amplifies at scale.

\paragraph{Practical value.} The $\alpha$ reversal signal is \emph{invisible to loss} (loss continues decreasing via memorization) but reveals that continued training at the current LR is structurally counter-productive. Detecting reversal enables:
(a)~adaptive LR decay (start cooling when structure saturates),
(b)~early stopping of wasteful training runs,
(c)~diagnosing under-capacity (model too small for the data diversity).

\subsection{P4: $\alpha$-Guided Adaptive Schedule}
\label{sec:schedule}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

We validate that $\alpha$ can \emph{guide} training decisions, not merely observe them, through a controlled experiment.

\paragraph{Setup.} Pythia-410M architecture, 25K steps, 2 seeds each:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Cosine}: standard cosine decay from step 250 (warmup end).
    \item \textbf{$\alpha$-Guided}: constant peak LR until $\alpha$ reversal detected (or 80\% fallback), then linear decay to $\eta_{\min}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Results.}
\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\caption{$\alpha$-guided vs.\ cosine schedule comparison. The adaptive schedule achieves both lower loss and substantially better spectral structure.}
\label{tab:guided}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Schedule} & \textbf{Final Loss} & \textbf{Final $\alpha$} & \textbf{Decay Start} & \textbf{Seeds} \\
\midrule
Cosine & $10.837 \pm 0.002$ & $2.94$ & Step 250 (1\%) & 2 \\
$\alpha$-Guided & $\mathbf{10.829 \pm 0.001}$ & $\mathbf{2.35}$ & Step 20,000 (80\%) & 2 \\
\midrule
$\Delta$ & $-0.008$ & $-0.59$ & & \\

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The $\alpha$-guided schedule maintains peak LR 80$\times$ longer than cosine (20K steps vs.\ 250), giving the model substantially more time for structure formation at high learning rate. The result: $\alpha_{\mathrm{final}} = 2.35$ vs.\ $2.94$---the guided model achieves deeper heavy-tailed self-regularization, and correspondingly lower loss.

This demonstrates the core prescriptive value: \textbf{spectral metrics can determine the optimal moment to begin LR decay}, replacing arbitrary schedule choices with a data-driven signal from the model itself.

\subsection{Structural Chinchilla Law}
\label{sec:scaling}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fitting $\alpha_{\mathrm{final}}$ vs.\ $D/N$ (tokens per parameter) across models $\leq$7B yields:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha(D/N) = 2.54 + 3.5 \cdot \exp\!\left(-\frac{D}{269 \cdot N}\right), \quad R^2 = 0.81
    \label{eq:structural_chinchilla}
\end{equation}

This ``Structural Chinchilla'' law quantifies the tokens-per-parameter budget needed for spectral maturity:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
    \item $D/N = 20$ (Chinchilla-optimal): $\alpha \approx 5.8$ --- \emph{structurally immature}
    \item $D/N = 269$ ($\tau$, characteristic scale): $\alpha \approx 3.8$ --- halfway to optimal
    \item $D/N = 500$: $\alpha \approx 3.1$ --- structurally mature
    \item $D/N \to \infty$: $\alpha \to 2.54$ --- asymptotic limit
\end{itemize}

The characteristic scale $\tau = 269$ tokens/parameter represents the budget at which heavy-tailed self-regularization is half-complete. Structure-optimal training requires ${\sim}12$--$25\times$ more tokens than compute-optimal (Chinchilla), explaining the empirical success of massive over-training strategies (Llama-3: 15,000 $D/N$; Pythia-70M: 4,261 $D/N$).

\paragraph{Size-dependent correction.} The OLMo-2-13B result challenges the fixed-$\tau$ formulation: at $D/N = 365$, Equation~\ref{eq:structural_chinchilla} predicts $\alpha \approx 3.44$, yet the observed $\alpha_{\mathrm{final}} = 6.95$---a discrepancy of $+3.51$. This reveals that the characteristic scale $\tau$ is not a universal constant but grows with model size $N$. Larger models have more parameters competing for gradient signal, requiring proportionally more tokens per parameter to achieve the same degree of spectral maturity. A revised formulation with $\tau(N) = \tau_0 \cdot (N / N_0)^{\beta}$ for some exponent $\beta > 0$ would accommodate this finding; fitting the precise scaling of $\tau$ with $N$ is ongoing work requiring additional measurements at 32B+ scale.

\subsection{Three-Phase Training Dynamics}
\label{sec:phases}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dense measurements from Amber-7B (360 checkpoints, 1.26T tokens) and OLMo-2-13B (25 checkpoints, 5T tokens) reveal three spectral phases:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Explosive ordering} (0--5\% of training, ${\sim}6$ tokens/param): $\alpha$ drops from ${\sim}18$ to its minimum at rate $\Delta\alpha/\Delta t \approx -2.3$/checkpoint. Both attention and MLP participate. SR drops 60\%+. For OLMo-2-13B, this phase spans steps 0--7K, with $\alpha$ dropping from 18.8 to 4.25.

    \item \textbf{Slow reversal} (5--80\%+ of training): $\alpha$ \emph{rises} from its minimum at rate $\Delta\alpha/\Delta t \approx +0.002$/checkpoint. MLP drives the reversal; attention remains stable. For OLMo-2-13B, the reversal spans steps 7K--596K (the \emph{entire} remaining training), with $\alpha$ rising from 4.25 to 6.95---the largest reversal magnitude observed ($\Delta\alpha = +2.71$).

    \item \textbf{Recovery} (only if $D/N$ is sufficient): $\alpha$ begins decreasing again. The accumulated gradient signal finally overcomes noise-driven degradation. OLMo-2-13B \emph{never reaches this phase}: even at $D/N = 365$ tokens/parameter, the model remains in Phase~2 throughout, confirming that larger models require substantially more data to achieve structural recovery.
\end{enumerate}

The ``7\% rule'': at the $\alpha$ minimum (typically 7--14\% of total training), models already achieve 83--100\% of their final downstream performance. The remaining 86--93\% of compute produces diminishing structural returns.

\paragraph{Ongoing experiments.} A real-data 3-way comparison (cosine vs.\ WSD vs.\ $\alpha$-guided scheduling on FineWeb-Edu, 10B tokens, Pythia-410M architecture, 2 seeds $\times$ 3 schedules) is currently running; results will be reported in the final version. This experiment tests whether the prescriptive value of $\alpha$-guided scheduling (Section~\ref{sec:schedule}) transfers from synthetic to real data at larger token scale.
