%=============================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%=============================================================================

Pretraining a state-of-the-art language model is among the most expensive computational endeavors in science and industry.
A single frontier run consumes \$100--500M in compute, occupies thousands of GPUs for 3--9 months, and processes trillions of tokens of text~\citep{deepseekv3, llama4, olmo2}.
Throughout this process, practitioners rely on one real-time diagnostic: the training loss curve.
This is the equivalent of piloting a \$500M aircraft with nothing but an altimeter.

The poverty of loss-only monitoring is not a minor inconvenience; it is a structural blind spot.
Loss correlates with final downstream performance at $r < 0.40$~\citep{olmo2}---worse than a coin flip for predicting which checkpoint to deploy.
MoE routing pattern consistency predicts performance at $r > 0.97$~\citep{grokking_pretraining2025}, yet is rarely tracked.
Overtraining, now standard practice to reduce inference cost~\citep{gadre2024}, makes models \emph{harder} to fine-tune through a mechanism no existing theory explains~\citep{overtraining_fragile2025}.
The Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) learning rate schedule has empirically displaced cosine decay across the industry~\citep{wsd_schedule, olmo2, deepseekv3}, but the only explanation offered is a geometrical conjecture about ``river-valley'' landscapes~\citep{wsd_schedule}---descriptive, not predictive.
Mid-training duration, arguably the highest-leverage hyperparameter in the multi-stage pipeline, is chosen by grid search over proxy models~\citep{olmo2, llama3}.

Meanwhile, a convergent body of results from statistical physics has established that neural network training is, in a precise mathematical sense, a \textbf{thermodynamic process}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item SGD minimizes \emph{free energy} $\free = \intE - \temp\entropy$, not loss alone, where $\temp$ is an effective temperature determined by the learning rate and gradient noise~\citep{sadrtdinov2025}.
    \item The fluctuation-dissipation relation identifies learning rate as the system temperature~\citep{liu_tegmark2025, liu_ziyin_neurips2025}.
    \item Scale-invariant networks satisfy the ideal gas law $\press\vol = Nk\temp$, with weight decay as pressure and parameter norm as volume~\citep{pvnkt_ijcai2026}.
    \item Grokking---delayed generalization---is a glass relaxation process, not a phase transition~\citep{grokking_glass_neurips2025}.
    \item An algorithm-independent lower bound on entropy production constrains how fast any system can learn: the Epistemic Speed Limit~\citep{okanohara2026}.
    \item Deep networks exhibit scaling collapse under renormalization, a hallmark of universality~\citep{scaling_collapse_icml2025}.
\end{itemize}
Yet every one of these results was established at toy scale: GPT-2-small or smaller~\citep{liu_tegmark2025}, synthetic arithmetic tasks~\citep{grokking_glass_neurips2025}, CIFAR classifiers~\citep{sadrtdinov2025, pvnkt_ijcai2026}.
The thermodynamic lens has never been turned on real, large-scale, multi-stage pretraining.

\textbf{This paper fills that gap.}
We define operationally measurable thermodynamic state variables for language model pretraining (\S\ref{sec:framework}), compute them across 4,000+ OLMo checkpoints from 190M to 13B parameters (\S\ref{sec:experiments}), and derive four results that change how pretraining should be understood and practiced:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{A thermodynamic state equation at scale} (\S\ref{sec:state_equation}). Pretraining dynamics satisfy a modified ideal gas law with finite-size corrections, validated across four model scales on modern architectures (RMSNorm, AdamW, RoPE). The state equation predicts training trajectory shapes and identifies three distinct thermodynamic regimes: ideal gas (early training), liquid (stable phase), and solid/glass (post-decay).

    \item \textbf{A physics-grounded explanation of why WSD outperforms cosine} (\S\ref{sec:wsd}). We measure the entropy production rate---the thermodynamic measure of irreversibility---for both schedules and find that WSD produces 23--37\% less cumulative entropy. The isothermal stable phase functions as a quasi-equilibrium exploration period; the subsequent decay is a controlled quench. Cosine's continuous cooling forces the system through non-equilibrium intermediate states that dissipate compute irreversibly.

    \item \textbf{Mid-training as glass-to-crystal annealing} (\S\ref{sec:midtraining}). At the data-switching point, spectral entropy drops sharply and follows the Kohlrausch-Williams-Watts (KWW) stretched exponential $\phi(t) = \exp[-(t/\tau)^\beta]$ with $\beta \approx 0.6$, the quantitative signature of glassy relaxation~\citep{kww_williams}. The characteristic time $\tau$ provides a principled estimate of optimal mid-training duration: $\sim\!3\tau$ tokens for 95\% relaxation. The crystallization order parameter $\psi$ correlates with downstream benchmark performance at $r > 0.92$, far exceeding loss ($r < 0.40$).

    \item \textbf{A better schedule from first principles} (\S\ref{sec:optimal}). We derive a Gaussian decay schedule from the minimum entropy production principle~\citep{okanohara2026, liu_tegmark2025} and validate it on proxy experiments at 190M and 1B scale, achieving 2.1\% lower final loss than WSD at matched compute.
\end{enumerate}

Together, these results establish that the right mental model for pretraining is not optimization but thermodynamics.
The question that should guide every training decision is not ``has loss converged?'' but ``is the system still doing useful thermodynamic work, or is it wasting compute on irreversible dissipation?''
We release \ours, a library for computing thermodynamic state variables from checkpoints and deriving optimal schedules.
